Tuesday on Wall Street was a day when two stories — inflation and Nvidia — added up to a modest but broad decline. The S&P 500 closed at {{px:^GSPC}} ({{chg:^GSPC}}), the Dow Jones at {{px:^DJI}} ({{chg:^DJI}}) and the Nasdaq Composite at {{px:^IXIC}} ({{chg:^IXIC}}). The small-cap Russell 2000 slipped to {{px:^RUT}} ({{chg:^RUT}}). Indices finished well above their lows: at one point in the early afternoon the Nasdaq was down as much as 1.7%.

What moved the market

The October consumer price index rose 0.4% month on month against economists' expectations of 0.3%. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3% versus a 0.2% forecast. According to Reuters, it was the third straight monthly headline gain of 0.4% or more. There are no comparable year-on-year rates for October, because data for October 2025 was not collected during the US federal government shutdown.

The Fed raised rates in September for the first time in three years. Before the release, Governor Christopher Waller said he favours another quarter-point hike in December and that a half-point move should be considered if monthly core inflation stays around 0.3% or higher. Markets priced that in quickly: according to CME FedWatch, as cited by Reuters, a December hike of at least 25 basis points is now fully priced, and the probability of a 50 bp move rose to 11.8%. The 10-year Treasury yield traded above 5% during the session and closed near 4.99%.

The second story was Nvidia. The stock fell {{chg:NVDA}} to {{px:NVDA}} USD and was the largest drag on the Dow. The company's independent directors said they had retained former federal prosecutor Joon H. Kim and law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz for an internal investigation into CEO Jensen Huang. The S&P 500 technology sector fell 1.4%, the weakest of the eleven sectors. The broader Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost only 0.4%, so the pressure was concentrated on one name rather than on the whole industry.

The week before: AI and the elections again

Tuesday has to be read against the days before it. On Thursday, 5 November, the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, its worst single day since April 2025 according to AP. The trigger was fear that Democratic wins in Congress and in state races could slow the build-out of AI data centres. The Russell 2000 lost 4.45% that day and the XBI biotech ETF 6.73% (Lupa calculation from Yahoo Finance closing prices). On Monday, 9 November, came a sharp rebound: the S&P 500 gained 2.35% and the Russell 2000 3.15%.

Why this matters for small caps

The most interesting number this week is not the daily decline but the relative performance. Over the month to 10 November the Nasdaq lost {{m1:^IXIC}} and the S&P 500 {{m1:^GSPC}}, while the Russell 2000 gained {{m1:^RUT}}. Biotech did even better: XBI rose {{m1:XBI}} and IBB {{m1:IBB}}. Money is not leaving the market as a whole; it is leaving the most expensive, most crowded AI-linked names.

Higher rates are still a headwind for small companies, for two reasons. The first is funding: loss-making companies, which dominate bioengineering and quantum computing, pay for their work by issuing new shares or taking on debt. When yields rise and volatility increases, raising money gets more expensive, and for existing shareholders that means more dilution. The second is valuation: the further away the cash flows, the more sensitive they are to the discount rate. A clinical-stage drug or a quantum roadmap targeting 2028 is worth less today at a higher discount rate, even if nothing else has changed.

Despite the sell-off, the VIX closed lower at {{px:^VIX}} ({{chg:^VIX}}). Over the month, however, it is up {{m1:^VIX}}, so the market is noticeably more nervous than it was in mid-October.

Quantum names: earnings and pressure

Among the themes Lupa covers, quantum stocks took the hardest hit, mostly because of their own results rather than macro. D-Wave reported third-quarter results on 10 November, with a conference call at 8:00 a.m. New York time, i.e. before the open: revenue of 4.5 million USD was up 21% year on year, but bookings for the quarter were only 1.9 million USD, down 19%. The stock fell {{chg:QBTS}}. Quantum Computing Inc. (QCi) reported a day earlier, and its stock lost {{chg:QUBT}}. Rigetti fell {{chg:RGTI}} during regular trading and reported after the close: revenue of 1.4 million USD missed analyst estimates, and so did its full-year revenue outlook of about 20 million USD.

What to watch

  • Rates. If the 10-year yield holds above 5%, companies that need fresh capital will feel the most pressure. We are watching for equity raises and at-the-market (ATM) share programmes.
  • Nvidia as a risk channel. Many small quantum and biotech companies highlight collaborations with Nvidia. A governance problem at Nvidia does not change their technology, but it can change how investors feel about the whole theme.
  • Calendar. Absci, Codexis and Twist Bioscience report this week (12 November, per stockanalysis.com); Infleqtion reports on 16 November.
