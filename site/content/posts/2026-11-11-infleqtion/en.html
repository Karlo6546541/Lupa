On the stock market, quantum computing is usually sold as a promise: qubits today, revenue some day. Infleqtion is different. The Louisville, Colorado company, known as ColdQuanta until 2022, is building a neutral-atom quantum computer, but most of its current revenue comes from selling quantum sensors and optical clocks and from government development contracts. It listed on 17 February 2026 after merging with the SPAC Churchill Capital Corp X, and it reports third-quarter results on 16 November.

The technology: atoms held by light

There are several ways to build a quantum computer. Rigetti and IBM use superconducting circuits cooled close to absolute zero, IonQ uses trapped ions, and Infleqtion — like QuEra, Pasqal and Atom Computing — uses neutral atoms. Individual atoms are held in an array by laser "tweezers", and their quantum states serve as qubits. The advantages are easy scaling of qubit counts and flexible connectivity; the weakness is slower operations. Rigetti's own release points out that its gates are about a hundred times faster than neutral-atom gates.

The company's quantum computer is called Sqale. The 2026 target is 30 logical qubits — error-protected qubits built from many physical ones — and according to the Q2 report the company is on track. For 2027 it is under contract to deliver a computer to the Illinois Quantum & Microelectronics Park, with an architecture designed to scale through modular upgrades to more than 50 logical qubits, on the way to a goal of 100.

The same physics — cold atoms and precise lasers — powers the sensing products: the Tiqker optical atomic clock, Quantum Spectrum RF sensors based on Rydberg atoms, and inertial and gravimetric sensors for navigation without GPS. There is software too: Superstaq, which optimises quantum programs, and Contextual Machine Learning, quantum-inspired AI software that runs on classical GPUs.

Ties to large companies and governments

  • NVIDIA: in October 2025 the companies agreed to deploy an NVQLink-enabled system in Illinois that couples Sqale with Nvidia GPUs. In April 2026 Infleqtion announced an integration of Sqale with Nvidia's Ising models for calibration and error decoding. According to the company, together with Nvidia it also published the first materials-science application run on logical qubits.
  • Eaton uses Sqale through private-cloud access for energy problems. Infleqtion's website also describes a multi-year programme with Eaton on power-grid contingency analysis, funded through an Air Force Research Laboratory award.
  • Safran is a global co-selling partner for Tiqker clocks. In late October, Tiqker ran continuously for two weeks aboard the US Navy support vessel RV Merlin during the Maritime Big Play Pacific exercise.
  • Honeywell Aerospace fabricated a prototype integrated optical cavity on a silicon-nitride chip in its photonics foundry (6 October 2026), a step towards hand-held sensors.
  • Leonardo is collaborating on a UK quantum RF direction-finding project, for which Innovate UK awarded £9 million on 13 October.
  • Government: NASA's Quantum Gravity Gradiometer programme was a major driver of Q2 growth. The company has three projects under the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, and the US Department of Commerce signed a letter of intent for up to 100 million USD of funding, with an equity stake in the company. At the Q2 report this was only a letter of intent, not a definitive agreement. Rigetti and D-Wave have since signed definitive agreements with the same department, so the status of Infleqtion's deal will be a key question on 16 November.

The numbers

Second-quarter revenue was 13.5 million USD, up 157% year on year. The originally reported 12.6 million was later revised for a change in the timing of revenue recognition on two government contracts, with a matching reduction in revenue for 2024 and 2025. All growth was organic and came entirely from quantum. GAAP operating loss was 29.9 million USD (10.4 million a year earlier) and non-GAAP operating loss 16.2 million. Revenue guidance for 2026 is about 45.1 million USD. According to TradingView, analysts expect third-quarter revenue of about 11 million USD and a loss of 0.05 USD per share.

Cash is the company's strongest card. The merger brought about 401.6 million USD from the SPAC trust and another 126.5 million from the PIPE. On 30 June the company held 582 million USD and had no debt. That figure includes 27.4 million USD of payroll taxes collected on option exercises, which were due to be remitted in Q3. Excluding that temporary effect, operating cash burn in Q2 was about 14 million USD.

A rough Lupa calculation: with a 2.11 billion market cap and about 555 million USD of cash after the withheld taxes are paid (30 June balance, before Q3 burn), enterprise value is about 1.55 billion USD. That is roughly 34 times 2026 revenue guidance. Expensive for any industry, but in quantum the multiple is lower than at Rigetti or D-Wave, which have less revenue and higher market values.

Risks

  • Technology timeline. Logical qubits have yet to show useful quantum advantage. The 2026 and 2027 targets are management promises, not results.
  • Government dependence. Much of revenue comes from government and defence contracts (NASA, the US military, the UK government). Budgets, revenue-recognition timing (already an issue in Q2) and heavy customer concentration make for lumpy quarters.
  • Dilution. There are warrants outstanding (INFQ WS), stock-based compensation is high, and a possible Commerce deal would give the government an equity stake.
  • Volatility. The stock is {{off52:INFQ}} below its 52-week high of {{hi52:INFQ}} USD and lost {{m1:INFQ}} over the past month. Quantum stocks trade as a pack: when Rigetti disappoints, Infleqtion falls too.
  • Competition. In neutral atoms it faces well-funded rivals, and in quantum computing generally, large tech companies building their own systems.

Lupa's take

Infleqtion is a rare quantum stock whose current revenue can be tied to a specific product: a clock aboard a ship, a sensor for NASA, an RF receiver for UK defence programmes. Sensors do not justify the whole valuation, but they give the company revenue and time while the computer is developed. With the 16 November results we will watch three things: whether the 45.1 million USD guidance holds, the status of the Commerce agreement, and how much cash was spent in Q3.
