Absci (ABSI): a key readout is close

{{quote:ABSI}}

What it does. Absci designs antibodies with generative AI and tests and refines its models in its own wet lab. Its lead candidate is ABS-201, an antibody against the prolactin receptor (PRLR), in a Phase 1/2a study for pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia). Interim data show it is well tolerated, and an estimated half-life of at least 65 days could allow two or three injections over six months.

Big-company ties. Eli Lilly invested 40 million USD in the company's shares in June, with no rights to the programme itself. Almirall expanded its collaboration to a second dermatology target, and AMD cites Absci as a user of its Instinct GPUs. The company says it has cash into the second half of 2028.

What to watch. Interim proof-of-concept efficacy data are due in the second half of 2026, so very soon, and full results in early 2027. It is a binary event: hair growth is slow to measure, and expectations are high. The stock fell {{chg:ABSI}} on 10 November, ahead of results on 12 November.

Sana Biotechnology (SANA): good science, short runway

{{quote:SANA}}

What it does. Sana engineers cells so that the recipient's immune system does not recognise them (its hypoimmune, or HIP, technology). In an investigator-sponsored study at Uppsala University Hospital, UP421 pancreatic islet cells survived and produced insulin for 14 months in a patient with type 1 diabetes, with no immunosuppression and no safety issues. The results were published as a letter in The New England Journal of Medicine.

Big-institution ties. A strategic collaboration with Mayo Clinic supports SC451, the stem-cell-derived version designed for broad use.

What to watch. Cash. Sana had 121.7 million USD on 30 September, with runway expected into mid-2027. Its positive Q3 net income (20.4 million USD) came from a non-cash change in the fair value of liabilities; the non-GAAP net loss was 43.0 million USD. Another equity raise is very likely; the question is at what price. An IND filing and Phase 1/2 start for SC451 are guided for this year, and first data for SG293 (in vivo CAR-T) for the first half of 2027.

Codexis (CDXS): a micro-cap at the one-dollar line

{{quote:CDXS}}

What it does. Codexis develops enzymes for drug manufacturing. Its BioCatalysis business sells enzymes used to make several commercially available small-molecule drugs. The main bet is ECO Synthesis, an aqueous enzymatic process for making siRNA drugs, as an alternative to conventional solid-phase chemical synthesis. At TIDES Europe on 9 November the company showed product quality above 90% at 100-gram scale, a cycle time nearly halved, and 3- to 4-fold higher potency for siRNA with defined stereochemistry in in vitro human liver-cell models.

Funding. According to RTTNews, Codexis signed a 60 million USD senior secured term loan with Trinity Capital, with 48 months of interest-only payments. The first tranche was used to retire existing debt.

What to watch. The stock closed at exactly {{px:CDXS}} USD ({{chg:CDXS}}). If it fell below one dollar and stayed there, a Nasdaq minimum-bid deficiency notice would become a risk. The key question for ECO Synthesis is whether a major siRNA drugmaker signs up to use it in commercial manufacturing. Results are due on 12 November.

Quantum Computing Inc. (QUBT): a cash pile bigger than the business

{{quote:QUBT}}

What it does. QCi is a photonics company: it makes thin-film lithium niobate chips and is developing the Dirac-3 optimisation machine. Its first-half 2026 acquisitions of LSI, NuCrypt and NHanced added photonic components, quantum-communications products and advanced chip-packaging services. Q3 revenue was 9.5 million USD, against 384 thousand a year earlier — but at a gross margin of only 4.5%.

Why it is interesting. The company ended the quarter with about 1.3 billion USD in cash and investments, and the market values it at about 1.43 billion. In other words, investors are paying very little for the operating business. Whether management deploys that cash wisely or burns it is the main question for shareholders. Its first pharma customer is INmune Bio, and contract backlog stands at 39.1 million USD.

Rigetti (RGTI): technical progress, weak quarter

{{quote:RGTI}}

What it does. Rigetti builds superconducting quantum computers from modular chiplets. Its Cepheus-1-108Q system (12 connected 9-qubit chiplets) reached a 99.5% median two-qubit gate fidelity, meeting its target. Next up is a 300-plus-qubit system at about 99.7% fidelity by mid-2028.

What disappointed. Q3 revenue was 1.4 million USD against a consensus of about 3.7 million. The roughly 20 million USD guidance for 2026 depends on India's C-DAC accepting a system in Q4. Shares fell about 8.7% after hours, according to Investing.com.

Government ties. In September it signed a definitive agreement with the US Department of Commerce for up to 100 million USD of CHIPS Act support, with a minority government stake in the company. Cash and investments are 497.1 million USD, with no debt.

D-Wave (QBTS): backlog up, quarterly bookings down

{{quote:QBTS}}

What it does. D-Wave sells quantum annealing computers (Advantage2) and is developing gate-model systems after acquiring Quantum Circuits in January 2026. Q3 revenue was 4.5 million USD (+21%), but new bookings in the quarter were only 1.9 million USD (−19%). Nine-month bookings reached 37.4 million USD; according to the earnings call, that includes a 20 million USD system sale to Florida Atlantic University.

Big-company ties. NTT DOCOMO put a second production application on D-Wave's technology into service, CGI will add D-Wave's platforms to its services, LG CNS signed a memorandum of understanding, and SkyWater has a multi-year manufacturing agreement. Cash is 503.4 million USD, plus access to up to 100 million USD of CHIPS Act funding.

What to watch. Remaining performance obligations (RPO) are 38.3 million USD, and about 59% should become revenue within 12 months. An adjusted EBITDA loss of 37.7 million USD per quarter is far larger than revenue.
